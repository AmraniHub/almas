## 1. About your closed test

**How did you recruit users for your closed test?**
(was: "i asked my friends and my family to test it and they were happy")
> I invited friends and family by email to join the closed test and install the app from Google Play. Several of them cook at home or buy spices for family events, which is who the shop sells to.

**How easy was it to recruit testers?** — keep the owner's earlier choice.

**Describe the engagement you received from testers.**
(was: "they believed that this app will be more beneficial for projects and restaurants")
> Testers browsed the catalogue, searched for products and built carts. They told us the app is most useful for businesses that buy in bulk, such as restaurants and event venues, which is why the new version adds ordering by the kilo and a business order form.

**Summary of the feedback, and how you collected it.**
(was: "liked it and they will always order spices in it")
> Feedback came directly from testers by WhatsApp and in person. They liked ordering spices from their phone and asked for the shop's real products and prices, larger quantities for businesses, and Arabic. Version 1.2.0 adds all three.

## 2. About your app

**Who is the intended audience?**
(was: "hotels and restarants because it is B2B")
> Customers of our spice and herb shop in Ksar Sghir, Morocco: households, and businesses that buy in bulk such as restaurants, hotels, caterers and wedding venues. The app is in Arabic, French and English.

**How does your app provide value to users?**
(was: "IT makes the order for spices and herbs faster")
> Customers see the shop's real products and prices on their phone, add products by the kilo or by the piece, and send a complete order to the shop on WhatsApp in a minute, instead of calling or coming to the shop to ask for prices.

**Expected installs in the first year:** 0 – 10K

## 3. Your production readiness

**What changes did you make based on the closed test?**
(was: "i changed the way it interacts with clients and the products appearance")
> We replaced the sample catalogue with the shop's 128 real products and prices, added 250 g quantity steps, a business order form that sends the order on WhatsApp, product photos, and Arabic. We removed the demo screens and fixed the layout of the product list.

**How did you decide the app is ready?**
(was: "because i will make more changes on it for my users")
> The app now shows the shop's real catalogue and the full order flow works end to end on a real phone: browse, add by the kilo, send the order to the shop on WhatsApp. Prices and photos update from the shop without an app update.

## 4. Additional testing

**What did you do differently this time?**
> This time the testers used the real app instead of a demo: the shop's actual products and prices, real ordering by WhatsApp, and Arabic. We tested the complete order flow on real phones and updated the store listing to describe exactly what the app does.
